Why bother

People are looking you up. Right now.

Before an interview, before a coffee, before saying yes to you — someone types your name into a search bar. LinkedIn is usually the first result.

The real point

You don't control whether people search for you. You control what they find.

What we'll do

Five things, in order

  • 1Look like a person. Photo, banner, location.
  • 2Say what you're about. The headline is the whole game.
  • 3Claim your address. Custom URL, thirty seconds.
  • 4Fill in the story. About and Experience.
  • 5Show up. Connect, comment, post.

Step 1

The photo is not optional

Do

Your face filling most of the frame. Good light. Plain background. Look at the camera. A phone is fine.

Don't

Group photos. Sunglasses. A cropped wedding picture. The default grey silhouette.

Banner

The wide image behind your photo. Empty blue says "I stopped halfway." Anything relevant beats nothing.

Step 2 · The most important line

Your headline follows you everywhere

It rides along with every comment, every search result, every message you send. It is not your job title.

Live example · linkedin.com/in/vanderpoolmichael ↗

“Life-long learner with a passion for helping people learn and grow”

No job title. No company. It says what he's for — and it reads the same whether he's teaching, consulting, or running a podcast.

Step 4

About: three short paragraphs

  • 1What you're doing now. Studying what, where, why it interests you.
  • 2What you've actually done. A project, a job, a club — with a detail that proves it.
  • 3What you want next, and how to reach you.
Write it like this

First person. Short sentences. Read it out loud — if you'd never say it, don't write it.

Step 5

Connections are the whole engine

Nothing you post reaches anyone until people are connected to you. Build the network first.

6,111followers on Michael's profile
500+connections

Built over years, one person at a time. Start with everyone you already know: classmates, professors, coworkers, family friends.

Step 5 · Connecting

Always add the note

A blank request is a coin flip. Two sentences makes it almost automatic.

Copy this shape

“Hi [name] — we met at the Junior Achievement workshop today. I'm studying [x] at [school] and would like to stay connected.”

Why it works

It answers the only question they have: who are you and how do I know you?

Step 5 · The easy part

Commenting beats posting

You don't have to write anything original to be visible. A real comment on someone else's post puts your name and headline in front of their entire network.

  • Add something — a question, an experience, a disagreement
  • Two sentences is plenty
  • “Great post!” is invisible. Skip it.
